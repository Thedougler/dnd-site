# Shattered Sea — Player Primer

> _Sixty thousand square miles of reef, jungle, and deep water. Three colonial powers have been failing to govern it for three hundred years. The people who were here first are still here. Something in the deep has started moving._

---

## The World

The **Shattered Sea** is a vast island chain — two arcs of limestone and basalt running east to west across open ocean, narrowing at the eastern edge toward a deep-sea trench called the **Drowned Maw**. The air here is warm and heavy before you see the first island: salt underlaid with something green, rainforest on the same wind that fills the sails. Then the cliffs appear, pale and high above turquoise reef.

The western entrance is guarded by the **Galewall** — a permanent storm belt. Three weeks through it lie the colonial homelands. Almost nobody goes back. Within the Sea, everything is measured in days of sail: Port Tidefall to Calveno is three days on good wind. Calveno to Kalowe, four. The eastern waters go dark and cold and the charts get thin.

The Sea has been contested terrain for three centuries. It was never unclaimed — the indigenous islanders, the Rattkin networks, the sea elves of the deep reef, the grung of the interior islands — all predate the colonial project. They remain, on their own terms.

---

## The Powers

Three colonial powers arrived in the Shattered Sea over the past three centuries. None of them has managed to fully govern it.

---

### The Dravosi Crown

The oldest colonial presence and the most visible. The Crown holds the **Central Strait** — the navigable corridor between the two island arcs — and taxes everything that moves through it. Rating officers in navy-blue coats open their ledger before they say hello, because the ledger is the point. Every ship that passes Dravosi waters generates a document, and every document is a thread of authority they can pull later.

They don't need to outfight you. They need to out-paper you, and they've been doing it for three generations. Fort Crestwall's cannon battery at Port Tidefall fires a morning salute across the harbour not because anyone is approaching — because everyone in the harbour should know, every morning, that the Crown has already won the argument about who this port belongs to.

---

### The Tessarine Concordat

The Concordat does not conquer. It lends. By the time a city realises it has a Tessarine problem, four of its seven council seats are servicing Concordat debt. Their factors dress like merchants — good fabric, plain cut, nothing that announces money — and extend credit with warmth. Collection, when it comes, is impersonal. It's not malice. It's arithmetic.

In Calveno, the oldest commercial city in the Sea, the Concordat holds leverage over six of the Seven Houses. The Council has been deadlocked four-to-three on Tessarine terms long enough that the three holdouts have started to look like the interesting ones.

---

### The Waveservants

The clergy of **Umberlee** — goddess of the sea in all its destructive capacity — maintain shrines at every port of any size and collect tribute from every ship that sails. Salt-bleached grey robes, always damp at the hem. They handle coin without looking at it.

Umberlee does not ask. She collects. Sailors who don't pay don't sail. The Waveservants are neutral to all factions by doctrine — a shrine that takes sides stops collecting from half the harbour, and half the harbour's tribute is not acceptable to the Bitch Queen. This arrangement predates every colonial power and has outlasted every flag that tried to reorganise it.

---

### The Passage

Not a nation. Not a guild. The **Rattkin** traveling network predates every colonial presence in the Shattered Sea. Runs of 20–60 individuals move between every major port, maintaining hidden supply caches called Holds inside every city's infrastructure — drainage conduits, wall cavities, dock substructure. The colonial powers cannot fully read them. That's the point.

The Dravosi Crown issues bounties for live Rattkin capture, calling it "relocation." These are almost never collected. The Tessarine Concordat quietly uses Rattkin couriers for off-record documents. Nobody discusses this in the open.

The Passage has one universal law: Rattkin do not move people in bondage.

---

### The Sentinels of the Eyrie

An Aarakocra martial monastery on a basalt sea stack off the northeastern Crown coast, predating all three colonial powers. The Sentinels have maintained an unbroken two-hundred-year observation record of the Drowned Maw — the most complete documentation of the deep eastern waters in existence.

Their doctrine is Objective Observation: record what you see, exactly as you see it, without conclusion or interpretation. The record is the product. What it means is not their question. They have declined every colonial request for alliance.

---

## The Sea Itself

**Port Tidefall** sits at the western mouth of the Central Strait, carved into three tiers of grey cliff face — Dockfront at the waterline, the Shelf mid-cliff, Fort Crestwall at the crown. Three nooses hang from the harbour arch. Nobody in port remembers when they went up.

**Calveno** is a canal city built from pale stone rising straight out of the water. Everything here is visible — deals struck in the open, information traded where witnesses can see. Beneath the oldest district, the only fixed Rattkin settlement in the Scatter has been running the Passage's anchor point since before the Seven Houses existed.

**Kalowe** is a free port built across three reef islands in the southern arc. No colonial administration. The place ships go when they can't go anywhere else.

**The Drowned Maw** is a deep-sea trench at the eastern convergence of both island arcs, 40–90 miles across. Compass drift begins within 5 miles of its edge. The water goes dark and cold before you reach it. The bottom is deeper than any chart records.

## People of the Sea

The Shattered Sea has been inhabited far longer than the colonial powers have existed. The following peoples are native to or long-established in the Scatter.

**Humans** arrived with the colonial fleets and now make up the majority of every major port's population. Their origins vary widely — Crown subjects, Tessarine merchants, free settlers, and those who came through the Galewall and stayed because they had no reason to go back.

**Rattkin** are Small, rat-like humanoids who can move through spaces no human can follow. Their skeleton is uniquely flexible — hinged ribcage, no collarbone — and their senses are calibrated for threat detection. They have been in every port in the Scatter longer than the buildings themselves. The Dravosi Crown classifies them as a pest species in three territories. The Rattkin do not appear to find this relevant.

**Aarakocra** are winged birdfolk who think in three dimensions. What a human sees as an impassable cliff is, to an aarakocra, a different altitude. They originate from the Elemental Plane of Air. In the Shattered Sea, the Sentinels of the Eyrie are the largest established community, though individual aarakocra move through every part of the Scatter.

**Grung** are brilliantly colored amphibious humanoids native to tropical rainforests. Their vivid hues — emerald, orange, blue, purple, red, gold — simultaneously signal lethal toxicity and denote rigid social caste. They are found primarily in the **Verdant Teeth** island cluster. Grung skin is poisonous to the touch and they must submerge in water for at least an hour each day or begin to deteriorate.

**Tabaxi** — catlike humanoids created by the divine Cat Lord — have inhabited the **Sunken Crown** islands for generations beyond counting, largely separate from the colonial ports. They are fast, perceptive, and built for hunting.

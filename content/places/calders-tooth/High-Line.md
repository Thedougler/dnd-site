# [[High-Line-Secrets|High-Line]]

A fishing hamlet built into the northeastern cliffs of [[places/calders-tooth/index|Calder's Tooth]], where the cold upwellings come in thick with yellowtail and the rock face drops straight to the water. There is no beach. There are no docks. What there is: massive counterweighted wooden winches bolted to the cliff edge, lowering nets down the face and hauling the catch back up.

The settlement is vertical — ladders, rope platforms, creaking stairways cut into stone, winch-baskets that double as the only transport between levels. The fisherfolk here know every handhold and wind angle. Visitors take longer to learn which gaps are fatal.

---

## Fauna

The cold upwelling that makes High-Line's nets worth the effort draws [[beastiary/sea-life/hunter-shark|hunter sharks]] into the water column below the cliffs — the same nutrient bloom that concentrates yellowtail concentrates everything that eats yellowtail. Winch operators have learned to read the surface for shark sign before sending repair crews down the net face on rope stages. The bottom of the cliff where wave action keeps a boulder field submerged holds [[beastiary/beasts/giant-crab|giant crabs]] that the settlement occasionally harvests when the surf is low enough to reach them, but the crabs are large enough that the harvesting crew goes armed.

The northern cliff faces carry [[beastiary/monstrosity/harpy|harpies]] — not every stretch, but close enough to High-Line's upper platforms that the hamlet posts watches during low-wind conditions, when the birds are more active and the winch operation is noisiest. An exposed worker on a rope stage forty feet above the water is exactly the kind of target that a harpy considers worth evaluating. The High-Line veterans wear safety lines not only for the fall.

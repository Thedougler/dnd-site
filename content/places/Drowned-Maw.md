# The Drowned Maw

![[raw/assets/banners/The-Drowned-Maw.webp]]

## Overview

The Drowned Maw is a north-south trench running along the eastern edge of the Verdant Scatter, 40 to 90 miles across. The approach is visible before any other landmark: the water changes colour from the blue-green of the Scatter shallows to something deeper and less defined, a blue with no bottom in it. The surface goes flat. Not calm — still, in a way that open ocean normally isn't. No current runs over it. Seabirds that follow ships across the Scatter turn back at the edge. No fish school over it. No sounding line has found the bottom.

Compasses drift within five miles and don't recover until you're through. Most captains navigate the crossing by stars and run it fast. Nobody anchors over the Maw. That particular piece of advice circulates without much explanation, and experienced sailors don't ask for one.

Ships cross routinely — it's the only route east to the [[places/Outer-Reach|Outer Reach]] — and most have no trouble. The ones that do have trouble come back quiet, which is its own kind of information. There are occasionally shapes visible at depth, in water that is clearer than it has any right to be this far out. Most crews don't discuss what they saw. Some do, once, and then stop.

---

## The Shelfworks

On the Maw's western rim, forty feet down, there are ruins: old stone buildings, dark-alloy fittings, construction that exceeds anything currently being made. The [[Shelfworks|Shelfworks]] are the current gold rush of the Scatter, with salvage crews on site most weeks. The upper shelf is accessible. What lies below it is not.

---

## Who's Been Watching

The [[High-Eyrie|Sentinels of the High Eyrie]] have kept aerial observation of the Maw's surface for two centuries. Their records are the most complete in the Scatter. The Waveservants dislike questions about what they think is down there, which tells you something, though not what.

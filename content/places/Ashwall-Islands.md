# Ashwall Islands

![[raw/assets/banners/Ashwall-Islands.webp|Dark volcanic islands rising from rough gray water beneath a stormy sky]]

## Overview

The water turns dark before the islands appear — cold, deep blue fading toward black, the warm green smell of the Scatter long gone. Then the spires rise: black volcanic stone, steep-sided, cloud dragging across the upper heights and surf bursting white against the bases. The air carries salt and something mineral beneath it, a thin sulphur bite from the vents in the high stone.

Sailors running the western passage know the Ashwalls as a threshold. Outbound, they are the last solid ground before the [[Galewall|Galewall]]. Inbound, they are the first proof of survival. Ashwall crews treat them accordingly — not as a destination, but as a reckoning point. The navigation tradition out here runs on stars and instruments and the kind of careful attention that keeps you alive when there is nothing ahead but open water.

The spires are not empty just because they are hard to land on. [[beastiary/giant-bat|Giant bats]] roost in warm vent caves, [[beastiary/giant-vulture|giant vultures]] work the cliffs after wrecks, and high-wind crews still tell [[beastiary/harpy|harpy]] stories about voices in the storm gaps. Most pilots file those stories under weather until someone follows the wrong sound inland.

The vent fissures in the high stone also hold [[beastiary/giant-scorpion|giant scorpions]]. The volcanic heat keeps the crevices warm through cold sea air, and the same crack systems that look like good handholds on the cliff face can run back several feet into occupied dark. A carpenter from a repair crew — name passed around Ashwall crews as Duvane — was attacked in a fissure while assessing a fouled spar: claw across the forearm, sting through the boot, two days down from the poison. He described not seeing it at all before both claws were engaged. Ashwall repair work now typically sends two hands up the stone face, with one watching the rock instead of the job. ^\[inferred]

## The Lightning-Burned Survivor

The Ashwalls are where Galewall stories become evidence. A ship that survives the storm belt reaches these spires damaged, wet, and short on certainty; pilots and repair crews can still read the hull better than the crew can read its own fear. Lightning-burned rope is common. Fused cleats, glassy pitch, and a mast split by something that moved sideways along it are less common.

One survivor from a failed crossing described a white bird made of stormlight moving through the rigging before the mainmast came down. The account is treated cautiously, but the Ashwall repairs gave it weight: burns ran along contact points rather than down from a single strike, and several iron fittings were magnetized hard enough to pull nails from a workbench. Since then, some Ashwall pilots mark bright, lateral stormlight as a possible [[beastiary/arclight-phoenix|arclight phoenix]] sign rather than ordinary weather. ^\[inferred]

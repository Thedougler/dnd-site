# Il Gioco delle Beffe

> _The game of pranks. The finest tradition in a city built on witnessed deals is the annual competition to make someone believe something that is not true — beautifully, publicly, and without lasting harm._

## Overview

**Il Gioco delle Beffe** — the Game of Pranks — is Calveno's most beloved annual festival, held across three days in the canal district. Teams design and execute elaborate public hoaxes on designated marks, judged by a committee of past winners on three criteria: craft (how well the deception was constructed), surprise (how completely the mark fell for it), and recovery (how cleanly the team walked it back). Lasting harm, property damage, and marks who did not nominally consent disqualify a team immediately. Everything else is fair.

The festival is old enough that its most legendary pranks are commemorated on a bronze plaque in the Velo Quarter. The current record-holder — a hoax in which an entire canal district was convinced that a famous privateer had died, triggering a spontaneous street wake that lasted six hours — has stood for forty years. The team responsible has never confirmed who they were.

Any group may enter. The entry stake is 25 gp per team, which goes into the prize pool. Teams with more money on the line tend to commit harder, which the judges consider a feature.

## How It Works

**Day One — La Scelta (The Choice)**

Each team registers their mark and declares their concept to the festival committee. The mark must be a willing participant — someone who has signed the committee's Beffa Accord acknowledging they may be the target of a prank this year, without knowing when or what. The Accord list is long; most of Calveno's merchant class signs it as a social gesture and then spends the festival week quietly nervous.

Teams do not have to reveal their concept in detail. They declare a category (Social, Environmental, Theatrical, or Compound) and a proposed scale (Minor / Notable / Legendary). A Legendary declaration raises the team's stake to 50 gp and their potential prize share accordingly. The committee loves Legendary declarations. They rarely work. When they do, the committee talks about it for years.

**Day Two — La Preparazione (The Preparation)**

Setup day. Teams prepare their prank without tipping off the mark. This is also the day teams may attempt to sabotage competitors — stealing props, planting false information, or tipping off someone else's mark (anonymously, always anonymously; getting caught sabotaging is a disqualification). The committee deploys three roving adjudicators who observe preparation and note what is being built. Roving adjudicators are famously bad at actually remaining unnoticed, which teams learn to exploit.

**Day Three — L'Esecuzione (The Execution)**

All pranks execute on Day Three. Teams do not coordinate timing, which means the day is chaotic from mid-morning onward: marks being misdirected through the Mercatura, canals suddenly full of improbable things, public announcements of events that are not happening. The committee watches everything. The crowd watches everything. Betting on which team wins runs hotter than the entry pool.

At sundown, each team presents their mark to the committee and walks through the prank. The mark gives a score out of five for how completely they fell for it. The committee scores craft and recovery. Combined scores determine the winner.

## The Prize

Entry stakes (25 gp or 50 gp for Legendary teams) pool together. The winning team takes 60% of the pool. Second place takes 25%. Third takes the remainder. The committee takes nothing — they are in it for the stories.

The Beffa Medallion, cast in bronze and depicting a laughing mask, is awarded to the winning team leader. It is the only award in Calveno that the Council families cannot buy, commission, or inherit. Past medallion holders are listed on the Velo Quarter plaque.

**The mark bonus:** By convention, a mark who was genuinely, completely, beautifully fooled pays their team a goodwill gift — typically 10–50 gp, sized to how impressed they are. This is not required. It is almost always paid.

## Notable Past Pranks

**The Six-Hour Wake (40 years ago)** — An unknown team convinced the Mercatura district that the privateer Caterina Luce had died at sea. Within four hours, a spontaneous wake had occupied the bridge plaza, three eulogies had been delivered by people who had never met her, and a Waveservant had begun planning a memorial. She was alive, in Calveno, watching from a second-floor window. She eventually descended to buy herself a drink. The judges scored it 29 out of 30 — the highest ever recorded.

**The Invisible Cargo (twelve years ago)** — A team convinced a Canal Commission inspector that a manifest described cargo that was invisible to anyone who had not paid their harbour dues. The inspector spent ninety minutes examining an empty hold and asking careful questions. Recovery was judged perfect.

**The Moustache of the Governor's Portrait (last year)** — A team added a moustache to the official portrait hanging in the Mercatura committee offices. The moustache was added via a duplicate portrait installed overnight; the original was in storage four buildings away. The judges scored it Minor but awarded full craft points. The committee keeps it up.

## Entering

The registration table opens on the Mercatura bridge on the morning of Day One. Entry requires the stake (25 gp standard, 50 gp Legendary), a team name, a mark selection from the Accord list, and a category declaration. Teams may register with as few as one person; there is no maximum.

The Accord list is available from the committee on request. It includes merchants, factors, harbour officials, clergy, and — this year — two members of the Council families, which the committee considers a notable development.

## Current Hooks

- Registration opens tomorrow morning. The prize pool is already rumoured to be substantial — several Legendary declarations are expected.
- The committee is openly hoping someone challenges the Six-Hour Wake's score this year. They have been hoping this for forty years.
- A prominent Tessarine factor signed the Accord this year for the first time. Teams who prank a Tessarine factor successfully tend to get interesting offers afterward.
- Giacomo Moretti has signed the Accord every year for twenty years and has never been pranked. He considers this a point of professional pride. The committee considers it an open challenge.

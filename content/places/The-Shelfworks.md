# The Shelfworks

![[raw/assets/banners/The-Shelfworks.webp]]

## Overview

You find the Shelfworks by following the boats. There is no island, no lighthouse, no harbour wall — only open ocean, a scatter of anchored vessels, marker buoys, pneumatic air-pump stations running lines over the side, and clear water that goes turquoise before it goes dark. The water above the shelf is startlingly clear. Sunlight reaches forty feet and illuminates pale stone buildings: walls still standing, archways intact, fish moving through rooms that once held people. From the surface it looks almost manageable. Then you notice where the shelf ends, twenty metres east of the last visible wall, and beneath that edge: nothing. Not dark water. Not a distant seafloor. Black, going down without a bottom.

The ruins are old and the material is not anything current forges produce. Salvage crews have been pulling dark-alloy fittings and composite stonework from the upper tiers for decades. A decade ago someone successfully reverse-engineered one of the mechanisms, and the operation has been a gold rush since. Crews work the forty-foot line on good weeks and push deeper when they feel like gambling. The shelf is accessible. The drop-off is not. Every crew at the Shelfworks has a story about the last crew that stayed too long below the line, and lately those stories have been coming back faster.
